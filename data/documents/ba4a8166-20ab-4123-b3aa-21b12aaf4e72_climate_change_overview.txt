Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperatures and weather patterns. 
These shifts may be natural, but since the 1800s, human activities have been the main driver, 
primarily due to burning fossil fuels like coal, oil, and gas.

When these fuels burn, they produce greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide and methane. 
These gases trap heat in the Earth's atmosphere, causing the planet to warm — a process known as the greenhouse effect.

Climate change has several consequences: rising sea levels, more intense storms, heatwaves, 
droughts, and biodiversity loss. It also affects food production, water supply, and human health.

To combat climate change, solutions include switching to renewable energy (solar, wind), 
improving energy efficiency, planting trees, and adopting sustainable agriculture. 
Global cooperation, as seen in agreements like the Paris Agreement, is vital for limiting global warming to below 1.5°C.
